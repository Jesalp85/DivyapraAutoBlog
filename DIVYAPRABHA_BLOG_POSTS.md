```html
<p class="dp-lead">
  When winter sets in across northern and western India, local markets fill with vibrant, plump, deep-red Banarasi chillies. This seasonal harvest marks the creation of one of India's most beloved winter delicacies: <strong>Bharwa Lal Mirch Ka Achar</strong> (Stuffed Red Chilli Pickle).
</p>

<h2>The Artisanal Preparation Process</h2>
<p>
  Creating an authentic stuffed red chilli pickle requires extraordinary patience and culinary skill:
</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Hand Selection & Slitting:</strong> Fresh, firm red chillies are hand-washed, dried under the sun, and carefully slit along the side while keeping the stem intact.</li>
  <li><strong>The Sacred Spice Stuffing:</strong> A fragrant mix of coarsely ground yellow mustard seeds, roasted fennel (Saunf), coriander seeds, amchur (dry mango powder), turmeric, hing, and pink rock salt is prepared.</li>
  <li><strong>Oil Binding:</strong> Raw cold-pressed mustard oil is mixed into the dry spices to form a moist, aromatic paste that is stuffed tightly into each chilli.</li>
  <li><strong>Jar Curing:</strong> The stuffed chillies are arranged vertically in glass jars and submerged in warm cold-pressed mustard oil to mature for 14 days under the sun.</li>
</ol>
```

```html
<h2>Sun-Cured Solar Magic vs Industrial Stove Cooking</h2>
<p>
  In modern commercial factories, sweet pickles are produced in under 30 minutes by boiling grated mangoes in high-fructose corn syrup or refined sugar solution over industrial burners. This artificial heat crystallizes the syrup, toughens the mango shreds, and destroys volatile heat-sensitive vitamins.
</p>

<h3>The 12-Day Rooftop Solar Infusion Ritual</h3>
<p>
  At DivyaPrabha Foods, Baa adheres strictly to the 100-year-old Saurashtra solar curing process:
</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Grating:</strong> Sour Rajapuri raw mangoes are peeled and shredded into long, uniform golden strands.</li>
  <li><strong>Solar Curing:</strong> The shredded mangoes are combined with organic jaggery syrup and placed in glass jars covered with fine muslin cloth under direct summer sunshine.</li>
  <li><strong>Daily Stirring:</strong> For 10 to 14 days, the solar heat gradually dissolves the sweet jaggery into the mango juices, creating a thick, naturally glossy syrup without a single drop of artificial thickener.</li>
  <li><strong>Spice Tempering:</strong> Roasted cumin seeds (Jeera), aromatic cinnamon bark, cloves, and Kashmiri chilli powder are folded in for a subtle sweet-spicy aroma.</li>
</ol>
```
